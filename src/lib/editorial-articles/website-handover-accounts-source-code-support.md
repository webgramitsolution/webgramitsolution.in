A good website handover leaves your business able to access, operate and recover the website with its own accounts. Ask for the agreed files, licences, documentation and support process before signing off. Assign responsibility for each account and test an everyday task with the people who will actually run the site.

## Verify ownership and account access

### Log in from company-controlled accounts

Verify the registrar, DNS manager, hosting provider and website or ecommerce admin. Use company-controlled email addresses, appropriate access roles and multi-factor authentication where available. Check the recovery phone or email and identify who receives notices about renewals, failed payments or changes to billing. A person leaving the business should not make it difficult to recover a critical account.

List connected tools such as analytics, Search Console, merchant centres, email providers, gateways, carriers and integration services. Some records are owned by a company account rather than a single login. Ask who can add or remove users and confirm the business owns the permissions it needs. Transfer access through account invitations or approved password management, and avoid copying passwords into handover documents.

### Keep a record of what controls each service

Document the domain expiry date, renewal owner and DNS changes required for the website or email. Record which provider hosts the storefront and which team can publish new changes. Note whether external services use the domain for verification. This list can save time when a renewal is missed or the business needs a migration.

## Collect files, source code and licence details

For a custom build, request the repository access agreed in the project scope, deployment instructions, build requirements and a list of external services the application calls. Ask how the team runs a test copy, how new credentials are provided and where release changes are tracked. Confirm which assets, custom work and content are owned by the business under the contract.

For a hosted platform, verify what can be exported and which elements are stored in the platform account. You may be able to export themes, catalogues and some settings while third-party extensions remain tied to subscriptions. Ask for an inventory of paid themes, plugins, apps, fonts and image licences. List their owners, renewal dates and transfer terms. Confirm whether any subscription remains in the agency’s account and how that arrangement is supported.

- Verify repository invitations and permissions if source code is part of the agreement.
- Obtain approved brand and design files in the formats you will need.
- Export product, page or order data according to your retention requirements.
- Record domains, apps and licences that renew automatically and who pays.
- Separate documentation from secrets and confirm access can be revoked.

## Run an operational walkthrough

### Let staff carry out real tasks

Have a member of your team sign in and perform an approved task in a safe environment. On an ecommerce site, update a variant’s stock, review a test order, verify shipping information and issue a test refund where supported. On a service website, edit a page, submit a test contact form and confirm it reaches the correct inbox. Confirm each person can access the functions they need with their own role.

Ask the developer to provide a short walkthrough and a checklist that staff can find after launch. Record what to do when a product or page fails to update, where to report an issue and how to recognise changes that need QA before publishing. Review which users can add accounts or grant permissions.

### Validate tracking and support handoffs

Check access to analytics and Search Console. Confirm the team can see key events such as enquiries or completed purchases without exposing customer data. Test the contact form and its backup path if staff do not receive an email. For paid campaigns, confirm which people can update key destination URLs and who monitors them after layout changes.

## Prove backups can be restored

Ask where backups are stored, what they include, how often they run and how long they are retained. A file copy may be useful without containing orders, customer records or configuration. Verify which data lives in third-party services and how you can recover it separately. Agree who can authorise a restore and what happens to data created after the recovery point.

Run a restore exercise where appropriate. Confirm a protected copy starts, administrators can sign in and relevant content or orders are present. Check that a test copy does not send real order messages or change live inventory. Attach the test date, responsible person and restoration notes to the handover package.

## Agree a post-launch support period

Define the start and end of the support period, what types of defects it covers and who responds when a service is unavailable. Clarify how a feature request becomes a new estimate and what information the support team needs when a problem occurs. For each external integration, note the owner, provider contact and any expected response or escalation steps.

As an example, a store may discover that prepaid orders are missing an email after a checkout update. The handover guide should identify the gateway and order record, where the pending job is visible and who investigates before staff contact the buyer. A useful guide turns the issue into a manageable task instead of leaving the merchant to guess which vendor is responsible.

## Final acceptance checklist

- Verify your team can sign in and recover each essential account.
- Confirm ownership, export access and renewal responsibility in writing.
- Run checkout, enquiry or other high-priority operational checks.
- Review backup and recovery notes with the support contact.
- Record outstanding issues, owners and dates before approving the handover.

Store the checklist with project agreements and share it with the people who will maintain it. For each open handover item, include its impact and a way to verify completion, such as a receipt for a licence transfer or a successful account recovery test. A handover succeeds when the business can perform key tasks, recover access and get help for a known issue.

### Schedule a follow-up after real use

Arrange a review after the team has processed its first orders or enquiries. Check whether staff can locate documentation, approve content changes and request assistance without sharing administrator access. Use their feedback to improve the handover notes, especially where operating instructions assume knowledge only the developer has. Record what remains open and when temporary developer access can be reduced or removed. A short follow-up also lets you confirm that alerts and usage reports reach the staff named in the original plan.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should my business own the domain and hosting accounts?
For most projects, the business should control the domain and have appropriate hosting or platform access. Agree the arrangement before launch and verify account recovery during handover.

### Do I always receive all source code?
That depends on the contract and third-party licences. Confirm what custom work, repositories, theme files and export formats you will receive before signing the project scope.

### What if a vendor app cannot be transferred?
Document who controls the existing account, any export options and whether the business needs a new subscription. Plan the transition and test the affected workflow.

### What should happen if a defect appears after launch?
Check the support terms, record the steps to reproduce the problem and use the agreed escalation channel. Keep critical checkout or enquiry issues separate from ordinary change requests.
